What got cut was what the frame asked of you

Now maybe you're a practitioner reading this and going, wait. Plenty of these methods are still in mainland TCM. So what got cut exactly? Fair question. Two things going on here.

First, the frame around the methods got swapped. Classical CM held all of this inside a Chinese cosmological and ritual context, Daoist and Buddhist and Confucian and folk-religious strands woven in, depending on the lineage and the period. The Siku 提要, the editors' summary at the head of the book, says the editors did not know who had gathered the collection, 其裒為一集不知何人所編, so the attribution to He Daren rests on the preface and the memorandum, which the Siku text itself prints at the head of the first juan. And for the load on one candidate, use the Chongning rule in the 宋史 itself: 次塲方脉試脉證運氣大義各二道, two 運氣 questions in the second session, and 鍼瘍試小經大義三道運氣大義二道, two more for the needling and sore streams. The Siku 提要 paraphrases it as 次塲方脈及臨證運氣各二道. That per-candidate figure is stronger ground than an anthology total, and it's the one the body uses. The 嘉祐 Jiayou-era promulgation of the 1050s and 60s, and Huizong writing 運氣 into the state compendium, both sit earlier and both are inference rather than examination text.

On the Yuan roster. The Yuan bureau set questions in thirteen departments, 十三科, in the words of the 元史, the official Yuan history (卷八十一, 選舉一), which names none of them. The lineage from 咒禁 to 祝由 is Lin's. And a roster is not the imperial examination, it's a bureau certifying its own staff, so when I say the state examined this, I mean the medical bureau, not that a man sat the 科舉, the imperial civil service examination, in exorcism.

On the 1822 strike. The Qing removal of the acupuncture department from the Imperial Academy of Medicine is conventionally dated 1822, on secondary sources.
